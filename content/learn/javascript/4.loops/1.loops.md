## What is a loop?

A loop is a way of telling the computer: **"Repeat this task again and again until a certain condition says stop."**

You write the instruction once, and the loop keeps running it for you. Every loop works on three ideas:

1. **Starting point**: where the repetition begins.
2. **Condition**: a question checked each time, such as "Should I keep going?"
3. **Change (update)**: something that moves forward each round so the loop eventually ends.

If nothing changes, the condition never becomes false, and the loop runs forever. This is called an **infinite loop**.


## Why do we need loops?

- **To avoid repetition:** Printing numbers 1 to 1000 by hand would mean writing 1000 lines. A loop does it with one instruction.
- **To handle unknown amounts of data:** You don't know in advance how many items a user will add to a cart, but a loop can process however many there are.
- **To automate tasks:** Checking every student's marks, sending a message to every contact, or searching through a list all rely on loops.
- **To keep code short and easy to maintain:** If you need to change the task, you change it in one place instead of hundreds.


## Types of loops, explained with real-life examples

### 1. `for` loop: when you know the count

**Example:** A teacher takes attendance for a class of 40 students. She knows exactly how many names to call, so she starts at roll number 1, goes up to 40, and stops.

### 2. `while` loop: when you only know the stopping condition

**Example:** You keep eating chapatis *while* you are still hungry. You don't know beforehand whether it will be 2 or 5. You check your hunger **before** each one.

### 3. `do...while` loop: runs at least once

**Example:** At a restaurant, you taste the food first and *then* decide whether to order more. The tasting always happens once, and the decision comes **after**.

### 4. `for...of` loop: going through values

**Example:** You open a fruit basket and pick out each fruit one by one: apple, banana, mango. You care about the **items themselves**.

### 5. `for...in` loop: going through labels (keys)

**Example:** You fill in a form with fields like Name, Age, and City. You go through each **field label** and look at what's written next to it.

### 6. `forEach`: doing the same action on every item

**Example:** A shopkeeper puts a price sticker on every product on a shelf. The same action is applied to each item, one after another.


## Controlling a loop

- **`break`: stop completely.** You're searching for your keys in 10 drawers. You find them in drawer 3, so you stop searching the rest.
- **`continue`: skip this round and move on.** You're checking a stack of letters and skip the junk mail without reading it, then continue to the next letter.

## Quick summary

| Loop | Idea | Real-life example |
|---|---|---|
| `for` | Known number of repeats | Calling 40 roll numbers |
| `while` | Repeat while a condition is true | Eating while hungry |
| `do...while` | Run once, then check | Tasting before ordering more |
| `for...of` | Go through each value | Picking fruits from a basket |
| `for...in` | Go through each key/label | Reading form fields |
| `forEach` | Same action on every item | Putting price tags on products |

**In one line:** a loop is the computer's way of doing repetitive work for you, stopping at the right moment, based on a condition you set.
